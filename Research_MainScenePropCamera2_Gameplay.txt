GAMEPLAY RESEARCH - "MainScene prop camera 2"  (cube-rail runner)
Date: 2026-09-30.  Nothing in the project was changed. A temporary probe script was used in Play mode and then deleted.
Scene file was never saved (not dirty).

HOW THIS WAS MEASURED
- Scene values were read from the scene file and the code (CubeRailRunner, DynamicPathGenerator + partials, CameraParentRotator,
  PathDepthShading, BridgePlankGuard, TurnFaceArrows, Coins).
- Live test 1 (survey): the AutoPlayer bot played the real scene with everything on (boosts, bridges, portals, decoys, coins).
  The bot notices a corner 1.3 s out and reacts in 0.2 s (0 s when under 0.8 s). It is near-perfect.
  Every leg, the camera every 0.1 s, every camera shot change and every death were logged. 2 runs: 512 legs, 576 s of play.
- Live test 2 (deadline trials, 520 trials): speed forced to 11.9 / 17.6 / 23.8 / 29.95 / 35.7 / 42.8 u/s.
  A tap was injected at a random time before the end of each leg, and we recorded whether the turn was made.
- Live test 3 (early-tap trials, 75 trials): a tap for the NEXT turn injected just before or after the corner,
  to see how early you may tap.
- "Human" numbers marked [LIT] are standard values from reaction-time and motor research, not measured here.
  Numbers marked [EST] are my estimates built from the measured windows plus the [LIT] numbers.

==========================================================================================
1. THE CORE RULE OF THE GAME (what the player actually has to do)
==========================================================================================
- The runner rides one of the 4 faces of a 1x1 square beam. Each leg ends in a 90-degree corner.
  The runner only makes the corner if it is on the face that points toward the next leg (the "turn face",
  shown by 4 yellow chevrons). Any other face: the runner flies off and the run ends 0.15 s later.
- After every corner the runner lands on the face pointing back along the old heading. From there the next turn needs:
    0 taps (already on the turn face)   18% of ordinary legs (measured)
    1 tap  (one 90-degree roll)         51%
    2 taps (two rolls = opposite face)  31%
  Boost legs: 91 of 117 needed 2 taps.
- Tap the left/right half of the screen (or swipe >= 50 px) = roll one face left/right on screen.
- IN THIS SCENE THE DOUBLE-TAP FLIP IS OFF (screenAxisControls = 1): two taps are two separate rolls.
  The second roll always waits for the first.
- Left/right is screen-relative. The mapping is chosen by the first press on each leg and held for that leg.
  flipRemapsControls = 1: once you have sat 0.3 s on the top<->bottom opposite of the face you landed on,
  left and right SWAP meaning.

==========================================================================================
Start speed: 11.90 world units/s (cubes per second). Authored 14 x overallSpeedMultiplier 0.85.
Hard ceiling: 35.70 u/s (42 x 0.85). Boost legs run x1.2, so the real top speed is 42.8 u/s.
Speed only changes per leg reached (score), never with time.

  leg 1000 35.69  (never quite reaches 35.70)

Curve shape: a "fast hook, then flatten" curve (concave), built from 3 straight-ish pieces plus an exponential tail.
  - legs 0-25:   about +0.29 u/s per leg (steepest; +24% in the first 10 legs)
  - legs 25-60:  about +0.19 u/s per leg
  - legs 60-100: easing from +0.19 to about +0.06 per leg
  - legs 100+:   exponential approach to 35.7 u/s, e-folding length 150 legs (half the remaining gap closes every ~104 legs)
  By leg 150 the speed is 88% of the way from start to ceiling. By leg 300 it is 94%.
  From about leg 150-200 on, the game is practically a plateau: speed rises under 1% per 10 legs.

==========================================================================================
3. LEG LENGTH AND TIME PER LEG
==========================================================================================
Leg length is a random whole number of cubes per tier (scene values):
  legs  0-15 : 14-18 cubes      (first leg of a run >= 20)
  legs 16-35 : 16-22 cubes
  legs 36-65 : 19-28 cubes
  legs 66+   : 22-34 cubes      (last tier, never grows again)
  No-double-murder rule: after a short leg (bottom 25% of the range) the next leg is drawn from the top 50%.
Special legs:
  Boost (zig-zag): every 12-16 legs, 7 legs. Middle legs are 12 cubes, or longer at speed (never under 0.4 s at the
    non-boost speed); first/last legs are 1.5x an ordinary leg. Runner speed is x1.2 on them.
  Bridge legs (after leg 25, 30% of ordinary legs): 1.5x long, or 2.1x for multi-plank. The plank is 20% of the leg
    and sits on ONE random face. Multi-plank legs (30% of bridges) have 1-3 planks, each on its own random face,
    with only 4 full cubes between planks.
  Portal every 30 legs: 15-cube runway in (must ride the top face) + 15-cube runway out.
  Decoy dead-end stubs (3-4 cubes) at 20% of corners after leg 20.

Time per ordinary leg (shortest / average / longest), from the tier range and speed:
  leg   0  1.18 / 1.34 / 1.51 s
  leg  10  0.95 / 1.08 / 1.22
  leg  20  0.91 / 1.08 / 1.25   (tier step: legs get longer, so time per leg goes UP again)
  leg  30  0.80 / 0.95 / 1.10
  leg  40  0.87 / 1.07 / 1.28   (tier step)
  leg  60  0.74 / 0.92 / 1.09
  leg  70  0.81 / 1.03 / 1.25   (tier step)
  leg 100  0.73 / 0.93 / 1.14
  leg 150  0.70 / 0.89 / 1.08
  leg 200  0.67 / 0.86 / 1.04
  leg 300  0.64 / 0.82 / 0.99
  cap      0.62 / 0.78 / 0.95
So the time pressure is a sawtooth (it drops, then jumps back at legs 16, 36, 66), and after leg 66 it slowly falls
from about 1.0 s to about 0.78 s per average leg.

  150 = 152 s, 200 = 194 s, 250 = 240 s, 300 = 280 s.  This is about 1 point per second throughout.

==========================================================================================
4. THE GAME'S OWN TIMING (fixed in seconds; does NOT scale with speed)
==========================================================================================
Measured with the deadline trials (threshold = the latest tap that still made the corner):
  1 tap   : must be PROCESSED >= 0.06 s before the end of the leg.
            11.9 u/s 0.063 s | 17.6: 0.065 | 23.8: 0.061 | 29.95: 0.065 | 35.7: 0.063 | 42.8 (boost cap): ~0.06-0.08
            In cubes before the end: 0.75 | 1.14 | 1.46 | 1.93 | 2.23 | ~3.4  (the distance grows, the time does not)
            0.04 s before the end ALWAYS fails. There is NO late grace once the runner reaches the edge.
  2 taps, queued instantly : first tap >= 0.16-0.18 s before the end (all speeds).
  2 taps, 150 ms apart (a normal human double tap): first tap >= 0.22 s before the end, so the second tap >= 0.07 s before it.
Early side (early-tap trials):
  - The runner "lands" on the next leg only 0.060-0.067 s (11.9 u/s), 0.027-0.033 s (23.8) or 0.015-0.022 s (35.7)
    before the visual end of the leg it is leaving.
  - A tap after landing is held: a 0.1 s landing lock means it starts rolling exactly 0.12 s after landing (6 physics steps).
  - A tap made up to ~0.03 s BEFORE landing is silently lost (no roll at all).
  - A tap earlier than that rolls the runner off the turn face of the current leg: death.
  => You cannot pre-tap the next turn before the corner. The window opens only at the corner.
Other fixed timings (code):
  roll animation 0.10 s (the runner is over the new face after 0.06 s); next roll after 0.10 s (cooldown 0.08 s = 5 physics
  steps); input queue holds 5 taps; physics step 0.02 s, so every timing above has +/-0.02 s jitter
  (= 0.71 cube at 35.7 u/s); off the track for 0.15 s = run over; touch fires on FINGER LIFT, not on touch-down.

Mechanical minimum leg time a corner needs (landing lock + rolls + 0.06 s deadline):
  1 tap  0.18 s     2 taps 0.28 s
  The shortest real boost legs at speed are 0.29-0.30 s, so a 2-tap boost leg has 0.01-0.02 s of mechanical slack.

So the per-leg TIME WINDOW a human has (when the tap must land, measured from landing on the leg):
  1-tap leg : from 0 (landing) to (leg time - 0.06 s)
  2-tap leg : first tap from 0 to (leg time - 0.22 s)  [150 ms double-tap]
  Examples:
                                         leg time   1-tap window   2-tap window (first tap)
  ordinary, start (leg 0-15)             1.18-1.51  1.12-1.45 s    0.96-1.29 s
  ordinary shortest, leg 100             0.73       0.67           0.51
  ordinary shortest, plateau             0.62       0.56           0.40
  boost middle leg, first boost (~13)    ~0.62      0.56           0.40
  boost middle leg, leg ~40              ~0.46      0.40           0.24
  boost middle leg, leg ~70              ~0.37      0.31           0.15
  boost middle leg, leg 100+             0.29-0.33  0.23-0.27      0.07-0.11   <- the hardest thing in the game

==========================================================================================
5. HUMAN NUMBERS [LIT]
==========================================================================================
  Simple visual reaction time (one known response to a signal): 200-250 ms typical adult. Fast gamers 150-200 ms.
    Online test medians are ~270 ms (they include device lag).
  Choice reaction time (decide left vs right, one vs two taps): 350-450 ms; more options and changing mappings make it slower.
  Touch latency on phones (finger to registered input to screen): 50-100 ms.
  Tap contact time (finger down to lift): 70-120 ms. Because this game fires on LIFT, it all adds to the delay.
  Fastest repeated tapping with one finger: 5-7 taps/s (140-200 ms between taps). A natural double tap: 120-200 ms gap.
  Timing precision when ANTICIPATING a predictable event (rhythm, an object arriving): standard deviation 20-50 ms.
  Errors: under time pressure even skilled players make roughly 1-5% wrong choices in 2-choice tasks.
    Mirrored or swapped mappings raise this a lot.
  So the real "decision to registered input" cost for a person:
    reactive, new player : 0.40-0.50 s choice + 0.07-0.12 s finger lift  = about 0.5-0.6 s
    reactive, skilled    : 0.20-0.25 s simple (already knows the move) + 0.07 s lift = about 0.3 s
    anticipated (rhythm) : 0 s of reaction, but +/-0.03-0.05 s timing error

==========================================================================================
6. CAN A HUMAN PLAY FOREVER? WHERE IT BECOMES TOO FAST
==========================================================================================
- Mechanically the game never becomes impossible. Speed stops at 35.7 (42.8 on boosts), leg lengths stop at 22-34 cubes,
  and every timing in section 4 is fixed. The bot reached score 320 (34.4 u/s, 4 min 55 s) without dying. Its other run died
  at 190 on a bridge plank: the bot left the plank face early to set up the corner, which a human could also do.
  After about leg 150-200 difficulty is stationary: every leg is the same distribution.
  Survival then works like a constant per-leg hazard: expected remaining legs = 1 / (failure chance per leg).
- Ordinary legs stay human-playable at the cap.
  The tightest case (2 taps on a 0.62 s leg) leaves a 0.40 s window. That is enough for a skilled player who already
  read the turn from the preview, since the next corner is drawn about 4 s ahead (see section 8).
  It is NOT enough for purely reactive choice: 0.5-0.6 s is needed vs a 0.40 s window. That crossover is around leg 100
  for the shortest 2-tap legs and around leg 250+ for average legs.
- Boost legs are where it becomes humanly too fast:
    first boost (legs 12-16): 0.40 s 2-tap window - fine.
    legs ~40: 0.24 s - already below reactive time; must be done by rhythm.
    legs ~70: 0.15 s - rhythm only; one slip of 0.05-0.1 s fails.
    legs 100+: 0.07-0.11 s - about 2-3x a person's timing error, at 6-7 taps/s (the limit of one thumb), for 5 legs.
  [EST] With a 40 ms timing error and a 90-140 ms window, each 2-tap boost leg succeeds roughly 70-90% of the time.
  Over 5 middle legs, a whole boost succeeds only roughly 20-60%. A boost comes every 12-16 legs (every ~12-15 s),
  so past leg ~100 even a very good player is expected to lose a run within 1-4 boosts.
- Bridges add a second ceiling. On multi-plank legs the next plank can be on the opposite face with only 4 cubes between.
  Two rolls need 0.16 s; 4 cubes last 0.20 s at 20 u/s, 0.13 s at 30 and 0.11 s at 35.7.
  So above ~25 u/s (leg ~50+), opposite-face plank pairs are mechanically almost impossible.
  The plank guard pauses during a roll, which gives only a sliver of extra room.
- Speed alone is NOT what limits humans; the short boost legs are, plus multi-plank bridges and the
  camera/control changes (section 9).

[EST] How long people last (score = legs, ~1 point per second):
  New player (reactive, still learning the face rule and screen-relative left/right):
    Legs 0-11 are easy (1.1-1.5 s per leg). The first boost (leg 12-16: 7 legs, 2 taps each) is the first big filter,
    then decoys (20+), bridges (25+) and the first portal (29).
    Typical runs end at score ~10-35 (10-35 s). An occasional good run reaches 40-60.
  Average player after some practice (has learned the boost rhythm and to read the chevrons early):
    ~40-90 (40-90 s). Legs 40-90 are where boost windows fall below reaction time.
  Pro / very skilled (fully anticipatory, two thumbs):
    ~100-200 (1.7-3.3 min). Past ~100 every boost is a 0.07-0.11 s window at 6-7 taps/s.
  Theoretical perfect play: unlimited. The bot did 320 legs (~5 min) without dying and only stopped because of my cap.

==========================================================================================
8. WHAT THE PLAYER CAN SEE AHEAD (anticipation)
==========================================================================================
- Track drawn: the current leg + 3 legs ahead (it draws itself in at 45 cubes/s) + 2 legs behind.
  Measured drawn track ahead (5% / 50% / 95%): 57 / 115 / 207 cubes = 2.7 / 4.0 / 7.6 s of running.
  The camera keeps 10 cubes and the next 2 corners in frame.
- Yellow chevrons on the turn face of the next 5 corners. The 4 chevrons sit at the corner end of the leg.
- So the information needed for a turn is available ~3-4 s early. What the player must react to is the moment of landing
  on the leg, not the turn direction.
- Coins: 50% of legs have a coin run; 70% of those runs are on a WRONG face (60% size), a deliberate lure.
  Coins stop 4 cubes before the corner. One apex coin per boost turn.

==========================================================================================
9. WHAT CAN ANNOY OR CONFUSE THE PLAYER (findings only)
==========================================================================================
- No late grace: a tap 0.04 s before the edge already loses. At speed the deadline is 2.2 cubes BEFORE the edge,
  so players will feel "I tapped in time" when they did not.
- Early taps are lost or fatal: a tap made just before landing does nothing, and an earlier one kills.
  No input buffering across a corner.
- The 0.12 s landing lock feels like input lag: a tap right after landing waits 0.12 s.
- Taps register on finger LIFT, adding 70-120 ms. Only the first finger on the screen is read: a second thumb that touches
  while the first is still down is ignored, and so is a tap that overlaps the first one's lift.
- Two taps = two rolls (no flip), so every half turn costs 2 taps 0.10 s apart; boosts need 6-7 taps/s.
- Controls change meaning: left/right is mapped to the screen and locked per leg. The camera rotates 90 degrees about every
  25 s. After sitting 0.3 s on the opposite (top<->bottom) face, left/right swap.
- The runner is small (20-27 px, 1.5-2% of screen height). It wanders between 15% and 87% of screen width, and the
  zoom pumps x1.3 on every boost.
- Hidden or random hazards: bridge planks on a random face, decoy dead-ends, wrong-face coins, the portal needing the top
  face, a 0-tap leg where a habitual tap kills you.
- Difficulty spikes rather than ramps: boosts arrive every 12-16 legs as a 7-leg burst of the hardest inputs.
  The first one comes as early as leg 12.
- Late game is static: after leg ~150-200 nothing new appears and speed barely changes. A long run becomes the same
  pattern repeated, with no new goal.

==========================================================================================
10. WHAT KEEPS THE PLAYER INVOLVED (findings only - how the current difficulty is built)
==========================================================================================
- Fast entry: +24% speed in the first 10 legs. A run reaches "flow" pace (~20 u/s) in ~30 s.
- Short, quick retry loop: run over 0.15 s after leaving the track, overlay at 1.0 s, tap lockout 0.6 s,
  path sinks/rises and camera rewinds (~2-2.5 s) and you are running again.
- Regular events at a human-friendly beat: a decision every ~1 s (50-60 per minute), a boost every ~12-15 s,
  a portal every ~30 s, the camera turning about every 25 s.
- New mechanics arrive in order: boost at 12-16, decoys at 20, bridges at 25, portal at 29.
  The turn mix rerolls every 5 legs, so stretches climb, dive or peel to one side.
- Pace breathers: no-double-murder (short leg, then long leg), longer legs at each tier step, longer first/last boost legs,
  and the 15-cube portal runways.
- Clear feedback: chevrons, lit boost legs, coins (with a risk/reward lure), apex coins on every boost turn,
  haptic on every roll, score = legs.
- Pure skill: no randomness in speed. Speed depends only on the leg number, so every run is "fair" and a better
  score means better play.
